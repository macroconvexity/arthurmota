How to read this page

R-star is the real interest rate that neither stimulates nor restrains the economy once it is at full employment with inflation on target. It is the benchmark against which a policy rate is judged tight or loose. Nobody observes it. It has to be inferred, and every method infers something slightly different.

This page combines four families of estimates: statistical models, what the bond market prices for the distant future, surveys and official projections, and the trend of real rates themselves. The useful reading is the range and where the families agree, not any single number.

The Banco Central do Brasil lens

The Banco Central do Brasil does not publish one neutral rate. It reports a set of measures and summarises them by their median and range. The same exercise is repeated here for the United States.

Limits

Model estimates have wide statistical bands, of a point or more on either side, and are revised as data are revised. Market measures move with risk appetite and with the supply of Treasuries, not only with views on the neutral rate. Survey measures move slowly and can lag. Trend measures follow policy itself, so they say where real rates have been, not where neutral is. Agreement across families is the only reassurance available, and even that can be shared error.
