How to read this page

Day to day, a currency follows three things more than any other: the gap between interest rates at home and abroad, the appetite for risk, and the price of oil. This page asks what the exchange rate would be if it followed only those three, and compares that with where it trades.

Fair value is that estimate. Gap is what is left: positive means the pair trades above what rates, risk and oil explain. The z-score expresses the gap in units of its own recent variation, so plus or minus two is unusual.

This is a description of the present, not a forecast. A gap can close because the currency moves or because the drivers do, and the relationship itself shifts over time.

Board

Spot changes in percent. Rate gap in percentage points, first currency of the pair minus the second. Gap in percent of fair value. Carry to vol is the rate gap divided by three-month realised volatility. REER is the real effective exchange rate of the non-dollar currency (the dollar itself for DXY) against its own ten-year average.

Limits

Three drivers do not explain a currency. Positioning, flows, fiscal news and intervention all sit in the gap. The link between a currency and its drivers changes with the regime and can take the opposite sign from the textbook for long stretches, as with the yen when its rate gap narrows while the currency keeps weakening. The relationship between rates and a currency is contemporaneous: it is positive day to day and tends to reverse at horizons of a year or more, so a rate gap is not a forecast of direction.
