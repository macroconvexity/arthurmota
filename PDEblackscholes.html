Black-Scholes PDE Lab

Research lab · Derivatives pricing, numerical methods

Solves the Black-Scholes partial differential equation on a grid with explicit, implicit and Crank-Nicolson finite differences, and checks every number against the closed-form price. Change the grid and watch the error, and the stability, respond.

Inputs

How to read this. An option's value depends on the price of the underlying asset and on the time left to expiry. At expiry the value is known: it is the payoff. The Black-Scholes equation says how that value changes as you step back in time, so a computer can start from the payoff and walk backwards, one small time step after another, on a grid of asset prices. For a plain European option there is also an exact formula, which makes it a clean test: the charts below show the grid answer, the exact answer and the gap between them. A finer grid shrinks the gap. One of the three methods (explicit) breaks down when the time step is too large for the price step, and the page tells you when that happens.

–Grid value at S0
–Closed-form value
–Error at S0
–Largest error on grid
–Explicit stability ratio (needs ≤ 1)
–Minimum Nt for explicit
–ΔS
–Δt (years)

Inputs are illustrative. The grid is uniform in S from 0 to Smax. The value at S0 is read off the grid by cubic interpolation, so S0 need not sit on a node.

Exhibit 6 Convergence: absolute error at S0 as the grid is refined (log scale)

Each row scales both NS and Nt by the factor shown, starting from your grid. Errors are grid value minus closed form at S0. Halving both steps should cut the Crank-Nicolson error by about four and the implicit error by about two. The explicit stability ratio doubles on each refinement, because Δt halves while ΔS² falls by four, so the explicit column is marked unstable once the ratio passes 1. The error ratio rows divide the absolute error on one grid by the error on the next finer one. They read n/a where either error is below 1e-7 of the strike or spot, where the error changes sign between the two grids, or where a run is unstable or skipped: the ratio of two near-zero errors carries no information.

Method and limits

Model. The underlying follows geometric Brownian motion with constant volatility σ, constant rate r and a continuous dividend yield q. Hedging the option with the underlying removes the randomness, and the hedged position must earn r. That gives the Black-Scholes equation for the value V(S, t):

∂V/∂t + ½ σ2 S2 ∂2V/∂S2 + (r − q) S ∂V/∂S − r V = 0

Conditions. At expiry the value is the payoff. With time to expiry τ = T − t the equation runs forward from that known start. At the edges of the grid the value is pinned to its known limit.

V(S, T) = max(S − K, 0) for a call, max(K − S, 0) for a put
call: V(0, τ) = 0, V(Smax, τ) = Smax e−qτ − K e−rτ
put: V(0, τ) = K e−rτ, V(Smax, τ) = 0

Payoff on the grid. The payoff has a kink at the strike, and sampling it at the nodes makes the answer depend on where the strike falls between two nodes. The solver starts instead from the payoff averaged over each cell of width ΔS. That changes one node only, the one whose cell holds the strike, and removes most of the kink error. It is why the expiry line in Exhibit 1 is very slightly rounded at K.

Discretisation. On nodes Si = i ΔS, central differences in S turn the right-hand side into a tridiagonal operator L with entries

ai = ½ (σ2 i2 − (r − q) i), bi = −(σ2 i2 + r), ci = ½ (σ2 i2 + (r − q) i)
(L V)i = ai Vi−1 + bi Vi + ci Vi+1

Time stepping. All three schemes are one formula with a weight θ:

(I − θ Δt L) Vn+1 = (I + (1 − θ) Δt L) Vn
θ = 0 explicit, θ = 1 fully implicit, θ = ½ Crank-Nicolson

The explicit scheme needs no solve but is only conditionally stable. The implicit and Crank-Nicolson schemes solve a tridiagonal system at every step with the Thomas algorithm (forward elimination, back substitution), which costs a number of operations proportional to NS. Explicit and implicit are first order in Δt, Crank-Nicolson is second order. All are second order in ΔS.

Stability of the explicit scheme. Each new value must be a non-negative weighting of the old ones. The binding node is the top of the grid:

Δt (σ2 NS2 + r) ≤ 1, that is Nt ≥ T (σ2 NS2 + r)

Doubling the price resolution therefore needs four times as many time steps. Central differencing of the drift also needs σ2 ≥ |r − q|, otherwise ai or ci turns negative at the nodes where σ2 i < |r − q|. With ordinary inputs that touches only the first node or two, where the value is nearly flat, and does no harm. With very low volatility it reaches further up the grid and values there can oscillate in any scheme, which the page flags.

Rannacher start. Crank-Nicolson does not damp the high-frequency error created by the kink in the payoff, which shows up as ripples in gamma near the strike when Δt is large relative to ΔS². Replacing the first two time steps with four implicit half-steps smooths the kink and keeps second-order convergence.

Benchmark. The closed-form price, with N the standard normal distribution function:

C = S e−qT N(d1) − K e−rT N(d2), P = K e−rT N(−d2) − S e−qT N(−d1)
d1 = (ln(S/K) + (r − q + ½ σ2) T) / (σ √T), d2 = d1 − σ √T

Limits. European exercise only: an American option needs an early-exercise check at every step, which this page does not do. Volatility, rate and dividend yield are constant. The grid is uniform in S, which wastes nodes far from the strike (a log-price or stretched grid is the usual fix). The upper boundary sits at a finite Smax, so a ceiling too close to the strike biases the answer, and a ceiling far above it stretches ΔS until the strike falls between nodes. The page caps Smax at 50 times the larger of strike and spot, warns when fewer than 12 nodes span three standard deviations (Kσ√T each) either side of the strike or when ΔS exceeds 10% of the strike, and always warns when the grid value at S0 misses the closed form by more than 0.1% of the strike, or by more than 1% of the closed-form value once the gap exceeds 0.01% of the strike. Under an unstable explicit run the charts hide values that have left the plotting range.
