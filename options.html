Method and limits

Model. European options on an underlying that pays a continuous dividend yield q, priced with Black-Scholes-Merton. S is spot, K the strike, T the time to expiry in years, r the continuously compounded risk-free rate, σ the implied volatility, N the standard normal distribution function and n its density.

d1 = [ ln(S/K) + (r − q + σ2/2)·T ] / (σ·√T)    d2 = d1 − σ·√T
Call = S·e−qT·N(d1) − K·e−rT·N(d2)
Put = K·e−rT·N(−d2) − S·e−qT·N(−d1)

Put-call parity. A call minus a put at the same strike and expiry is a forward, whatever the volatility. The page checks this on every recalculation.

Call − Put = S·e−qT − K·e−rT

Greeks. Analytic, in the units a screen usually quotes. Vega is per one volatility point (0.01), rho per one rate point (0.01), theta per calendar day (1/365 of a year). The stock leg has delta 1 and no other Greek.

Delta: call e−qT·N(d1), put −e−qT·N(−d1)
Gamma = e−qT·n(d1) / (S·σ·√T)
Vega = S·e−qT·n(d1)·√T / 100
Theta call = [ −S·e−qT·n(d1)·σ/(2√T) − r·K·e−rT·N(d2) + q·S·e−qT·N(d1) ] / 365
Theta put = [ −S·e−qT·n(d1)·σ/(2√T) + r·K·e−rT·N(−d2) − q·S·e−qT·N(−d1) ] / 365
Rho: call K·T·e−rT·N(d2) / 100, put −K·T·e−rT·N(−d2) / 100

Expiry profile. At expiry a call is worth max(ST − K, 0) and a put max(K − ST, 0). The profit and loss is the sum over legs minus the net cost today. That function is piecewise linear with kinks at the strikes, so breakevens, maximum gain and maximum loss are solved exactly on the segments between strikes, not read off a grid. A position whose payoff keeps rising or falling beyond the last strike is reported as unlimited.

P&L(ST) = Σ quantityi · payoffi(ST) − Σ quantityi · pricei(S, today)

Limits. One flat volatility for every strike and expiry, so no skew or term structure: spreads and strangles are priced as if both strikes traded at the same implied volatility. European exercise only, so early exercise of American options is ignored. No bid-offer, commissions, margin or financing of the premium. Dividends received on a stock leg are left out of the expiry line. The spread strategies use the lower of the two strikes as the low strike whichever slider it comes from. The normal distribution function uses the Abramowitz and Stegun 7.1.26 approximation, accurate to about 1.5e-7, which puts prices within roughly 0.00002 for a spot of 100.
