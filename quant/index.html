Table Sharpe ratio by starting year: does the result depend on where the sample begins?

The chance that the gain is real comes from a block bootstrap of the difference in Sharpe ratios. The deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and Lopez de Prado) asks how likely it is that the true Sharpe ratio is above zero once you allow for the number of ideas tried. Trying many ideas and keeping some produces a good-looking backtest by luck alone, and this is the correction for it.

Limits

A backtest is not a track record. The sample starts in 2008. Several strategies are long equities part of the time, so the book has a correlation of about one half with the S&P 500 and part of its return is equity risk taken at chosen moments. It loses sharply when volatility jumps. Effects of this kind can fade once they are widely known, and some depend on trading at very low cost. ETF closing prices are not always tradable in size.
