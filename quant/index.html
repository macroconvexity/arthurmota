How to read this page

This is a paper portfolio run by rules, with no discretion. Nine strategies trade liquid ETFs across equities, rates, commodities, currencies and volatility. Every trading day the page records what the book holds, what it traded, its risk and its result.

Two families. Risk premia are returns paid for bearing a risk others avoid. Flows and calendar are returns that come from when large investors have to trade.

It is simulated. No money is invested. The simulation runs from , and everything before is a backtest: rules applied to history after the fact. From that date on the record is out of sample, because the rules were fixed before the returns happened. The two are shown apart.

Returns are in excess of the 3-month Treasury bill, after trading costs. Not investment advice. No positions are recommended.

Limits

A backtest is not a track record. The sample starts in 2008. Several strategies are long equities part of the time, so the book has a correlation of about one half with the S&P 500 and part of its return is equity risk taken at chosen moments. It loses sharply when volatility jumps. Effects of this kind can fade once they are widely known, and some depend on trading at very low cost. ETF closing prices are not always tradable in size.
